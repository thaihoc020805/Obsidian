# Grammar Quick Reference

Only concise rules that actually came up in practice. Keep each item short: **rule → contrast → example**.

## Time and frequency

### three times a week
Use **times** after numbers greater than one; use singular **week** in the pattern `a week`.
- ✅ three times a week
- ❌ three time a weeks

### for the past + period
Use for a situation that started in the past and continues until now.
- ✅ I haven't exercised regularly for the past three years.
- Usually combine with the present perfect.

### over the past + period
Good for changes or developments during a recent period.
- ✅ Over the past three months, I have started focusing on my health.

## Present vs past

### can't vs couldn't
Use **can't** for a current/general difficulty; **couldn't** for a past situation.
- ✅ I can't wake up early.
- ✅ I couldn't wake up early yesterday.

## Gerunds and verb patterns

### such as + noun / V-ing
After **such as**, use a noun or gerund form.
- ✅ such as swimming or playing badminton
- ❌ such as go swimming or play badminton

### go for a run
`go for a + activity noun` often means go and do that activity for a period of time.
- go for a run = đi chạy bộ
- go for a walk = đi dạo

### take a + noun
Common chunks where **take** does not mean “cầm/lấy”.
- take a walk
- take a break
- take a shower
- take a look

## Adjectives

### -ing vs -ed
- **refreshing / relaxing** = something causes the feeling
- **refreshed / relaxed** = a person feels it

Examples:
- Running in the morning is refreshing.
- I feel refreshed after running.

### energetic vs energizing
- **energetic** = full of energy; usually describes a person
- **energizing** = gives energy; can describe an activity

## Relative clauses

### comma + who
Use commas for extra, non-essential information.
- ✅ My PT, who teaches me how to exercise properly, is helpful.

No comma when the clause identifies which person you mean.
- ✅ The PT who teaches me is very helpful.

## Prepositions

### on a trip
Use **on** with `trip` when talking about something that happened during a trip.
- ✅ on my trip to Cat Ba
- ✅ on my Cat Ba trip
- ❌ in my trip

### at school / at work
Use **at** for the general activity/place context.
- at school
- at work

## Comparison

### much + comparative
**much** can strengthen a comparative.
- much fresher
- much more energetic

Do not use **much** to mean frequency.
- ✅ I don't do it very often.
- ❌ It isn't much. (if the intended meaning is “not often”)

## unlike
Safest pattern for writing:
- **unlike + noun / gerund phrase**
- ✅ unlike running in the evening

`unlike in the evening` can appear as an elliptical structure, but it is less clear and is better avoided in Aptis writing.
